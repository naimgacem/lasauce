\section{Reading the canvas}

Three features of this model deserve to be stated rather than left to be
inferred from the grid.

\paragraph{Only the result is paid for.} Nobody pays to report, to search, to
browse or to claim, and finders --- the scarcer side of the market --- never pay
at all. The one thing sold is the engine's work: the identity of a report it
believes describes the same object, offered to the person who lost something,
at the moment their motivation is highest and only once a result exists. The
first unlock is free, so every user sees the product work before being asked
for money, and a locked suggestion still shows its confidence, so the buyer
knows what they are paying for. The paywall sells convenience, not access: every
report remains publicly browsable and searchable, so a person willing to search
by hand can still find their object without paying, and a suggestion confirmed
by both parties is never locked.

\paragraph{The asset is the pool, not the software.} The matching engine could
be rebuilt by a competent team; the accumulated corpus of reports, and the
labelled record of which suggestions owners confirmed and which they rejected,
could not. That corpus is what improves the ranking over time, and it compounds:
more reports produce more suggestions, more suggestions produce more verdicts,
and more verdicts produce better matching. It is also why the found side must be
filled first, through institutions, and why reporting must stay free.

\paragraph{Low costs make a low price possible, but not a large income.} Because
both models run on the CPU of a 4\,GB server, an unlock can be priced like a
coffee and still leave 96\,\% of the average purchase after fees. The same
arithmetic sets the limit of the model: at about ten dinars per report, the
paid tier pays for the platform early but pays salaries only at a national
volume. The canvas therefore keeps institutional accounts as an option: the
institutions already receive value from the pool, and charging them would add a
second stream without taxing the people the pool depends on.
