\section{Reading the canvas}

Three features of this model deserve to be stated rather than left to be
inferred from the grid.

\paragraph{The paying customer is not the user.} Citizens never pay, on either
side. This is the only configuration in which the engine receives the data it
needs: a fee on the loss report suppresses demand from people who have just lost
something, and a fee on the found report suppresses the scarcer side of the
market outright. The institutions that pay do so for a different good --- a
searchable register and reduced counter load --- which happens to be produced by
the same pool.

\paragraph{The asset is the pool, not the software.} The matching engine could
be rebuilt by a competent team; the accumulated corpus of reports, and the
labelled record of which suggestions were confirmed and which rejected, could
not. That corpus is what improves the ranking over time, and it compounds:
more reports produce more matches, more matches produce more feedback, and more
feedback produces better matching. It is also why reaching density first
matters so much --- being second in this market is close to being absent.

\paragraph{The cost structure is what permits the price structure.} A platform
paying an external provider per embedding could not give the service away.
Running open models locally on CPU turns the marginal cost of one more report
into a fraction of a cent of storage, and that is what makes a permanently free
consumer service defensible in front of a jury rather than aspirational.
