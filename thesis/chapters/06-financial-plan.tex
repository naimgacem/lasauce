\chapter{Financial Plan}
\label{ch:finance}

\section{Basis of the plan}

This chapter prices the platform as it was built and measured in
Chapter~\ref{ch:prototype}, not as a hypothetical company. Its costs are the
monthly subscriptions and levies the project would actually pay, at public
prices consulted in late September 2026 and cited line by line. Its revenue
comes from the one thing the prototype sells --- unlocking a match suggestion
(Section~\ref{sec:paid-matching}) --- at the prices written in its code. Where a
quantity cannot be measured before launch, such as how many people will report
an object in a month, it is stated as an assumption and varied across three
scenarios rather than given as a single figure. Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}
lists the assumptions.

\section{Operating costs by phase}

\subsection{Phase 0 --- the demonstration: 0~DZD per month}

Until the defence, the platform runs on Oracle's Always Free ARM server, which
since 2026 provides two cores and 12\,GB of memory at no
charge~\cite{terminalbytes2026}. The deployment kit of
Chapter~\ref{ch:prototype} needs nothing else: the free \code{sslip.io} service
gives the server a hostname, Caddy obtains a free HTTPS
certificate~\cite{caddy2026}, payments run in simulated mode, and notifications
stay in-app, so no e-mail service is needed. A bank card is required to open the
account, for identity verification only.

This phase is free but not dependable. Oracle reclaims an Always Free server it
considers idle --- one whose processor, network \emph{and} memory use all stay
under 20\,\% for seven days~\cite{oraclefree2026} --- and the measured footprint
of 1.2\,GB is only 10\,\% of the 12\,GB on offer. A quiet week is enough to lose
the server. The allowance itself was halved in 2026 without much
notice~\cite{terminalbytes2026}. Both are acceptable for a demonstration that
can be redeployed in minutes from the kit; neither is acceptable for a service
that holds people's reports and takes their money.

\subsection{Phase 1 --- the public pilot: about 4\,900~DZD per month}

The pilot moves to a paid server and opens real payments, which requires a
registered activity. The auto-entrepreneur status is the lightest one available:
its tax (IFU) is 0.5\,\% of turnover with a minimum of 10\,000~DZD per year, its
social contribution (CASNOS) is a flat 24\,000~DZD per year, and it covers
turnover up to 5\,000\,000~DZD~\cite{autoentrepreneur2026} --- far above any
scenario in this chapter. Table~\ref{tab:phase1-costs} lists every recurring
cost.

\subsection{Phase 2 --- a company with salaries: about 131\,500~DZD per month}

Two events end the auto-entrepreneur phase: turnover approaching the
5\,000\,000~DZD ceiling, or the need for the Startup Label, which is granted only
to companies~\cite{startuplabel2026}. The label exempts the company from profit
tax (IBS) for four years, renewable for two more, and gives priority access to
public startup financing. Forming a two-partner SARL costs 283\,610~DZD through
a turnkey provider, including the notary, the commercial register, the legal
publication and twelve months of domiciliation, plus a capital of
100\,000~DZD~\cite{creation2026}. Table~\ref{tab:phase2-costs} gives the

\section{Revenue model}

\subsection{What is sold}

Reporting, searching and claiming are free; what is sold is the identity of a
suggested match, at the prices of Table~\ref{tab:packs}. Every account receives
one free unlock, so a person pays only from their \emph{second} suggestion
onwards. Revenue per report is therefore the product of three factors:

\begin{equation}
\text{revenue per report} \;=\; o \times b \times \bar{p},
\label{eq:revenue}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $o$ is the share of reports that receive more suggestions than
the free allowance covers, $b$ the share of those owners who buy, and $\bar{p}$
the average purchase net of the payment fee --- 404.7~DZD under the
auto-entrepreneur status (Table~\ref{tab:fees}), and 337.6~DZD once the company
also charges VAT.

\subsection{The three factors}
\label{sec:revenue-factors}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Opportunity, $o$.} Measured on the evaluation corpus, 18 of 56
        reports (32\,\%) have two or more live suggestions
        (Section~\ref{sec:eval-matching}). That corpus was built to exercise
        the matcher and is denser in matching pairs than real traffic will be,
        so 32\,\% is used only as the favourable value; the cautious and base
        scenarios use 15\,\% and 25\,\%.
  \item \textbf{Buying rate, $b$.} No measurement exists before launch. The
        scenarios use 5\,\%, 10\,\% and 20\,\% of the owners who see a locked
        suggestion. The pilot's first task is to replace these figures with
        observed ones; the platform already records every unlock and purchase.
  \item \textbf{Average purchase, $\bar{p}$.} Assuming half of purchases are
        single unlocks, 40\,\% packs of 3 and 10\,\% packs of 10, the average
        purchase is 420~DZD for 2.7 unlocks, which leaves 404.7~DZD after the
        payment fee.
\end{itemize}

With these values, one report brings 3.0~DZD of net revenue in the cautious
scenario, 10.1~DZD in the base scenario and 25.9~DZD in the favourable one.
Figure~\ref{fig:breakeven} plots the resulting revenue against the cost levels
of the next section.

\section{Financing}

The demonstration costs nothing and the pilot year at most 59\,000~DZD, so
neither needs external money: the founders can finance both. Financing becomes
a question at Phase~2, which needs the company's formation (283\,610~DZD), its
capital (100\,000~DZD) and a runway; six months of Phase~2 costs add
788\,850~DZD, for a total of about 1.17 million DZD. That is the scale of the
schemes run by ANADE, which reach 10 million DZD~\cite{anade2025}, and of the
public startup financing to which the Startup Label gives priority
access~\cite{startupdz,startuplabel2026}. The pilot's measured conversion rates
are what such an application would need to show.

\section{Conclusion}

Three conclusions follow from these numbers.

\paragraph{Running the platform is cheap, and the reason is measured.} A
platform with two AI models costs under 2\,000~DZD a month in infrastructure,
because both models run on the CPU of a 4\,GB server and photographs are
compressed to tens of kilobytes. The fixed costs of being a registered business
are larger than the cost of the software.

\paragraph{Revenue per report is small.} At the base assumptions a report
brings about ten dinars. The paid tier covers the pilot's running costs from
around 500 reports a month in the base case, but paying even one person at the
minimum wage requires about 2\,900 reports a month, and a salaried team of two
about 15\,600 --- a national volume, not a single wilaya's. Growth must
therefore be geographic, and a second revenue line, such as accounts for
institutions that hold large numbers of found objects
(Chapter~\ref{ch:business}), may be needed before the team can be paid.

\paragraph{The risk is commercial, not technical.} Every uncertain figure in this
chapter is a volume or a conversion rate. The pilot's purpose is to measure
those two numbers, and the platform already records everything needed to do so.
