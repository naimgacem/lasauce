\section{The Project Idea}

Losing a personal object is an ordinary event with a disproportionate cost. A
wallet contains identity papers whose replacement takes weeks; a phone carries
years of photographs; a set of keys can lock a family out of its own home. The
object itself is rarely the largest loss.

What makes this problem tractable is that the information needed to solve it
usually already exists. In a large share of cases somebody \emph{did} pick the
object up, and would return it. The two halves of the solution --- a person
searching and a person holding --- are both present, often within the same city
and the same week. What is missing is any mechanism that compares them.

In Algeria today, recovery relies on three channels, and each fails in the same
structural way:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Physical lost-property desks} at transport hubs, universities
        and municipalities. Each keeps its own register, none of them shared, so
        the search space a citizen can actually cover is one desk at a time, in
        person, during opening hours.
  \item \textbf{Social-media groups}, where a report is a piece of unstructured
        text that scrolls out of visibility within hours. Nothing indexes it,
        nothing filters it by category or place, and nobody rereads last month's
        posts against today's.
  \item \textbf{Word of mouth}, which does not scale beyond a neighbourhood.
\end{itemize}

The common failure is not a shortage of goodwill or of information. It is that
no actor in the system is \emph{responsible for comparing} a loss report against
a discovery report. Two matching descriptions can coexist, at the same moment,
in the same city, and never meet.

\projectname{} takes on exactly that responsibility. Users report a lost or a
found item through one guided form --- a description in their own language, a
category, a colour, a brand, a wilaya, a date, and photographs --- and the
platform searches the opposite pool on their behalf, continuously and
automatically. The comparison is performed by artificial intelligence on two
channels at once: the \emph{meaning} of the written descriptions, and the
\emph{visual appearance} of the photographs. Each candidate pair receives a
confidence score together with the reasons that produced it, and when a pair is
strong enough both people are notified.

Two design commitments distinguish the result from a simple classified-ads
board. First, matching is \textbf{multilingual by construction}: a description
written in Arabic is comparable with one written in French or English, because
the underlying language model encodes meaning rather than words --- an
indispensable property in a country where all three languages circulate freely
and often within a single sentence. Second, \textbf{a match never reveals
anybody's contact details}. Recovery passes through a verification handshake in
which the person who reported the item sets the questions, reviews the answers,
and decides. Identities are exchanged only after that approval.

The conditions for such a service exist. Algeria counted 36.2~million internet
users at the start of 2025, a penetration of 76.9\%~\cite{datareportal2025},
and the market is decisively mobile: of 59.10~million internet subscriptions
recorded by the regulator in the second quarter of 2025, 88.71\% were mobile
against 11.29\% fixed~\cite{arpce2025}. A web platform reachable from any
smartphone browser, with no installation, addresses that population directly.

\section{Proposed Values}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=5pt]
  \item \textbf{Recovery instead of search.} The citizen stops being responsible
        for canvassing desks and groups. One report enters a pool that is
        searched automatically, in both directions, for as long as the report
        stays open.

  \item \textbf{Comparison across languages.} A loss described in Arabic and a
        discovery described in French are matched on meaning. No user is
        penalised for the language they are most comfortable writing in.

  \item \textbf{Comparison without words.} A photograph is evidence in its own
        right. A finder who writes three words but attaches a clear picture
        still surfaces to the right owner.

  \item \textbf{Explained suggestions rather than opaque ranking.} Every
        suggestion carries a confidence percentage and the reasons behind it, so
        the user applies judgement the system does not have.

  \item \textbf{Privacy by default.} No telephone number, address or e-mail is
        ever exposed by a match. Contact is the outcome of an approved claim,
        never of an algorithm.

  \item \textbf{National coverage.} Every wilaya is supported, and proximity is
        part of the matching signal. A platform for lost property is worth
        little if it only works in the capital.

  \item \textbf{Accessibility.} The service is free to report and free to
        search, works on an ordinary smartphone browser with no installation,
        and is designed to remain usable on a modest connection --- which
        matters in a market where nine subscriptions in ten are mobile.

  \item \textbf{Cost structure that permits a low price.} All inference runs
        locally on open pre-trained models, so serving one more user costs
        infrastructure only. There is no per-request charge to any external AI
        provider, which is what keeps reporting free and lets a match
        suggestion be unlocked for a few hundred dinars.
\end{itemize}

\section{Project Objectives}

\subsection{Short-term objectives (first 12 months)}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{Put the platform into production and run a pilot.} Deploy
        publicly and operate a supervised pilot on one campus --- Akli Mohand
        Oulhadj University of Bouira --- before opening nationally. A university
        is the right first ground: it concentrates thousands of people in a
        bounded space where objects are lost daily and where a lost-property
        desk already exists to partner with.

  \item \textbf{Solve the cold-start problem deliberately.} A matching platform
        has no value until both pools are populated, and the found side is
        always the scarcer one. The first year therefore prioritises
        \emph{supply}: signing institutional partners whose existing registers
        seed the found pool, rather than advertising to individuals who have
        nothing to match against yet.

  \item \textbf{Reach an operational volume of reports.} Target a first-year
        volume sufficient for matching to produce suggestions routinely rather
        than occasionally, concentrated in the pilot wilayas so that density ---
        not raw national totals --- carries the match rate.

  \item \textbf{Measure real matching quality.} Instrument confirmation and
        rejection rates, precision at the notification threshold, and the
        proportion of reports that end in a recorded recovery. These are the
        figures that justify the product, and none of them can be obtained from
        a demonstration corpus.

  \item \textbf{Replace hand-tuned scoring with learned scoring.} Use the
        confirmations and rejections accumulated during the pilot to fit the
        fusion weights and calibrate confidence into a true probability, as
        described in Chapter~\ref{ch:prototype}.

  \item \textbf{Open real payments.} Register the activity under the
        auto-entrepreneur status, open a merchant account with the payment
        gateway, and switch the paid matching tier from simulated to live
        payments, at the costs set out in Chapter~\ref{ch:finance}.

  \item \textbf{Build presence at the moment of loss.} Demand for this service
        is event-driven: nobody looks for a lost-property platform until
        something is lost. Search visibility therefore matters more than
        sustained advertising.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Long-term objectives (two to five years)}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{Extend coverage across the wilayas}, prioritising the dense
        urban ones where loss events and population concentrate, and where the
        66.8\% of Algerians living in urban areas are found.

  \item \textbf{Integrate institutional registers.} Move partner lost-property
        desks --- universities, transport operators, airports, municipalities
        --- from private registers onto the shared pool, so that a citizen's
        single report is compared against institutional holdings automatically.

  \item \textbf{Ship a native mobile application}, enabling a report to be filed
        from the camera at the moment of discovery, which is when a finder is
        most willing to act.

  \item \textbf{Extend the matching engine to new categories} --- documents,
        vehicle papers, and eventually lost pets --- each of which shifts the
        balance between the textual and visual signals and benefits from the
        per-category weighting already provisioned.

  \item \textbf{Become the reference platform for lost property in Algeria},
        measured not by user count but by the number of objects actually
        returned to their owners.

  \item \textbf{Sustain the service financially} without charging anyone to
        report or to search: revenue comes from unlocking match suggestions,
        analysed in Chapter~\ref{ch:finance}. When salaries become necessary,
        incorporate and obtain the national Startup Label granted under
        Executive Decree No.~20-254~\cite{startupdz}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Project implementation schedule}

The platform was built in incremental milestones, each one shippable and
demonstrable on its own, so that a working system existed at every point rather
than only at the end. Figure~\ref{fig:schedule} shows the schedule actually
followed: architecture and design first, then infrastructure, authentication and
item management --- at which point the product already functions as a
classified-ads board --- and only then the embedding pipeline and the matching
engine that give it its value.

The ordering is deliberate. The artificial intelligence is the last thing built,
not the first, because it operates on data the rest of the system produces. An
embedding pipeline with no items to embed cannot be tested, and a matching
engine with no corpus cannot be evaluated.
